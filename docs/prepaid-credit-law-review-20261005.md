# Prepaid Laundry Credit: Legal Research

Reviewed October 5, 2026. Research and implementation guidance, not a legal
opinion or a representation that counsel has approved the terms. The program
assumptions are Indiana-only Clean Stream locations, reloadable customer-owned
credit, no gifting, transfers, outside purchases, or routine cash withdrawal.

## Refunds After 48 Hours

No general Indiana statute requiring a merchant to cash out this type of unused
credit merely because its holder changes their mind was identified. The 48-hour
unused-load refund is a voluntary benefit, not a statutory cooling-off period.
That conclusion is not a finding that every possible refund can be refused.

- Indiana Code 24-5-0.5-4(c)(2) and (d) permit court-ordered restitution for
  deceptive consumer transactions and permit affected contractual clauses to be
  voided or limited. A no-refund clause cannot establish immunity from those
  remedies. An equipment failure alone is not automatically a deceptive act;
  the facts and statutory elements matter. [Statutory text](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-24-trade-regulation/in-code-sect-24-5-0-5-4/).
- Regulation Z 12 CFR 1026.13(a)(1), (a)(3), and (b)(1) covers certain unauthorized
  credit-card charges and services not delivered as agreed, with billing-error
  notice generally due to the creditor within 60 days of the relevant statement.
  This is an issuer dispute process, not a general merchant cash-redemption rule.
  Importantly, commentary 13(a)(3)-2 distinguishes a contemporaneous service
  purchase from loading an intermediary balance for later use. Do not promise
  that every later laundry dispute reverses the earlier wallet load automatically.
  [CFPB rule and commentary](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/13/).

## Expiration and Deletion

Federal 12 CFR 1005.20(e) generally protects covered gift-card funds for at least
five years. But subsection (b)(2) excludes reloadable products not marketed or
labeled as gifts. Clean Stream may fit that exclusion on the stated facts;
the actual product and marketing must match. A dollar-denominated wallet is not
automatically exempt simply because its terms call it prepaid service. Nor is
paid principal a free promotional reward. [CFPB gift-card rule](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20/).

Indiana Code 32-34-1.5-3(12) defines its unclaimed-property gift-card category using
nonexpiring value and restricted redemption. Section 3(24) excludes that category
from reportable property but includes certain other credit balances, refunds,
and overpayments. This is a classification/exemption, not a blanket statutory
command that every wallet must last forever. Making a balance expire can change
its classification; it does not automatically make it the merchant's money.
[Indiana definitions](https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-32-property/in-code-sect-32-34-1-5-3/).

For property that is subject to the Act, section 36(a) says a contractual or
statutory limitation on recovery does not eliminate abandonment/reporting duties.
Consequently, a deletion-forfeiture term is not a proven way to bypass those
duties. [Section 36](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-32/article-34/chapter-1-5/section-32-34-1-5-36/).

The official 2026 Indiana site was reached, but its virtualized section body did
not render for extraction; the identified statutory text above was read in
FindLaw's January 2026 reproduction and Justia's 2025 section 36. Do not present
that as a complete October 2026 legislative-history or case-law opinion.

## Implementation Decision

The terms now say loads are prepayment for participating laundry services only,
generally nonrefundable and not cash-redeemable, except the chosen 48-hour policy
or a legally required remedy. Same-day on-site reporting governs the voluntary
machine-failure remedy, not statutory disputes. The Owner confirmed a same-day
resolution target while the customer is washing, subject to staff availability.
A timely report remains eligible for later reconciliation/approval if staff
response is delayed. Unavailable replacement machines leave a claim
open; the policy does not silently extinguish the underlying claim.

The Owner subsequently expressly directed voluntary-deletion forfeiture after
the 90-day recovery deadline and no cash redemption outside the existing refund
and claim policies. The app/web review drafts now state that business rule with
a nonwaivable-law exception. Deletion does not create a new refund right, extend
deadlines or cancel timely claims. This supersedes the earlier draft's promise
of continuing ordinary credit access after deletion, not existing acceptances
or the implemented ledger/cleanup contract.

Being a non-bank does not prevent a merchant from issuing a refund; Stripe
supports merchant refunds to the original payment method. The draft therefore
states a purchase/nonredemption policy, not a legal inability to refund.
[Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds).
No finding above establishes universal enforceability of the forfeiture clause.
An Indiana-specific applicability review remains necessary before financial
enforcement; describing value as prepaid services alone does not settle it.
